EDITORIAL PROCESS

Independent assessment from submission to decision.

Expanding Horizon Journal uses editorial screening followed, where appropriate, by double blind peer review. The process is designed to protect scholarly independence, fairness and confidentiality.

01

Submission and receipt

Authors submit the manuscript, title page and required declarations through the journal submission system. The editorial office records the submission and checks that the files are complete.

02

Editorial screening

An editor assesses scope, basic scholarly suitability, originality concerns, ethical requirements and readiness for review. A manuscript may be declined at this stage when it is outside the journal's scope or does not meet basic standards.

03

Reviewer selection

Suitable manuscripts are sent to appropriate independent reviewers. Reviewers are selected for relevant expertise and should disclose conflicts of interest.

04

Double blind peer review

Where double blind review is used, reviewer and author identities are concealed from each other as far as practicable. Reviewers assess the manuscript's quality, originality, evidence, reasoning, clarity and contribution.

05

Editorial decision

Reviewer reports inform the editorial decision. Possible outcomes include acceptance, minor revision, major revision or rejection. The editor remains responsible for the final decision.

06

Revision and publication

When revision is invited, authors respond to the editorial and reviewer comments and submit a revised manuscript. Accepted articles proceed to the journal's publication workflow and receive final bibliographic information when published.

CONFIDENTIALITY & INTEGRITY

Protecting the scholarly record.

Editors and reviewers are expected to treat manuscripts and associated information confidentially. Editorial decisions should not be influenced by author identity, institutional status, nationality, gender, political position or other irrelevant characteristics.

Concerns about plagiarism, fabrication, falsification, duplicate publication, authorship or other research integrity matters may be investigated under the journal's publication ethics policies.
